Our editorial approach

From a lead to a reliable record.

Every story should make clear what is known, how it was checked, and what remains uncertain.

  1. 01

    Listen

    Understand the concern, who is affected, and what is already known.

  2. 02

    Verify

    Check records, dates, definitions, and other possible explanations.

  3. 03

    Investigate

    Follow the paper trail, examine incentives, and seek relevant responses.

  4. 04

    Publish

    Separate documented facts from analysis and show where claims come from.

  5. 05

    Correct

    Keep a route open for corrections and explain changes to the record.

Evidence before emphasis

Give context for figures and claims. Explain their sources, limits, and what the available material does not establish.

People beyond statistics

Include lived experience with care and context, without reducing people to numbers or case studies.

Accountability both ways

Make uncertainty visible, invite correction, and help readers trace the evidence behind a story.

Consumer Rights: Challenging Unfair Terms in Online Casinos

Surveys disputes involving contract terms and bonus conditions, and the options players may have to challenge them.

BIGJN AdministratorApr 17, 2026
Read guide
Consumer Rights Guide

Unfair Terms Exposed

What Makes a Term Unfair?

The article discusses the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 and terms that may create a significant imbalance against consumers.

Common Concerns

  • Wagering conditions that are not clearly disclosed.
  • Terms that allow an operator to close accounts unilaterally.
  • Excessive chargeback fees.

Possible Steps to Challenge a Term

  1. Keep records, including screenshots, emails, and account communications.
  2. Raise a complaint with the relevant regulator, such as the UKGC or MGA where applicable.
  3. Consider qualified legal advice where significant losses are involved.

This is general information, not legal advice. Consumer protections and complaint routes depend on the jurisdiction and circumstances.

Beyond Self-Exclusion

Self-exclusion and deposit limits alone may not provide enough protection. The article calls for a broader approach to how operators design safeguards.

The Illusion of Control

Deposit caps and self-exclusion registers are often treated as primary defenses. The article argues that relying on them alone can leave important gaps.

What Could Help

  • Personalized risk scoring: Use AI-assisted behavioral analysis to identify risk patterns earlier, with appropriate privacy safeguards.
  • Cooling-off periods: Require breaks after defined loss thresholds.
  • Staff training: Help staff recognize potential harm patterns and respond promptly.

AML Failures in the iGaming Sector: A Data-Driven Investigation

Reviews anti-money-laundering controls, suspicious activity reporting, and oversight in online gambling.

BIGJN AdministratorApr 26, 2026
Read investigation
Data-Driven Investigation

AML in iGaming: Systemic Failures

The report states that 67% of operators fail to meet basic AML standards.

Methodology

Analysis of Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) from 2022–2024 across major jurisdictions, including the UK, Malta, and Gibraltar.

Findings

  • Average SAR filing rate: 0.2% (the report states a benchmark of 2–3%).
  • 42% of operators have no dedicated AML officer.
  • Customer due-diligence gaps in 78% of cases.

Example query

query_sar_failures.sql

SELECT operator_name, jurisdiction,
    COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
    SUM(CASE WHEN sar_filed = true THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) as sar_count,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN sar_filed = true THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) as sar_percentage
FROM compliance_data
WHERE transaction_amount > 10000
GROUP BY operator_name, jurisdiction
HAVING sar_percentage < 0.5
ORDER BY sar_percentage ASC;
