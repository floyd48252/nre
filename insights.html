AML in iGaming: Systemic Failures

Examines suspicious activity reporting and anti-money-laundering controls across major jurisdictions.

View findings

The report says 67% of operators fail to meet basic AML standards.

Methodology

The report describes an analysis of Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) from 2022–2024 across the UK, Malta, and Gibraltar.

Reported findings

  • Average SAR filing rate: 0.2%, compared with the report's stated 2–3% benchmark.
  • 42% of operators reportedly have no dedicated AML officer.
  • Customer due-diligence gaps were reported in 78% of cases.

Example query

query_sar_failures.sql

SELECT operator_name, jurisdiction,
    COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
    SUM(CASE WHEN sar_filed = true THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) as sar_count,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN sar_filed = true THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) as sar_percentage
FROM compliance_data
WHERE transaction_amount > 10000
GROUP BY operator_name, jurisdiction
HAVING sar_percentage < 0.5
ORDER BY sar_percentage ASC;

These are claims presented in the report. The underlying dataset was not supplied with this summary, so the figures have not been independently verified here.

A cross-national report on social costs, treatment access, and player-protection rules in 15 European markets.

Why Responsible Gambling Tools Are Failing a Generation

Looks beyond self-exclusion and deposit limits toward earlier, more responsive safeguards.

BIGJN AdministratorApr 14, 2026
Read policy note

The article argues that self-exclusion and deposit limits are reactive tools and should not be the only protections available.

Suggested measures

  • Personalized risk scoring: Identify concerning behavior earlier, with appropriate privacy safeguards.
  • Cooling-off periods: Require breaks after defined loss thresholds.
  • Staff training: Help staff recognize potential harm patterns and respond promptly.

Consumer rights

Challenging Unfair Terms in Online Casinos

A UK-focused overview of disputed contract terms, bonus conditions, and possible complaint steps.

BIGJN AdministratorApr 17, 2026
Read policy note

Issues discussed

The article references the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 and terms that may create a significant imbalance against consumers.

  • Wagering conditions that are not clearly disclosed.
  • Terms that allow an operator to close accounts unilaterally.
  • Excessive chargeback fees.

Possible steps

  1. Keep records, including screenshots, emails, and account communications.
  2. Raise a complaint with the relevant regulator, such as the UKGC or MGA where applicable.
  3. Consider qualified legal advice where significant losses are involved.

General information, not legal advice. Consumer protections and complaint routes depend on jurisdiction and circumstances.

The Case for Public Benefit Corporations in Gaming

Explores whether corporate governance can balance financial aims with stated social objectives.

BIGJN AdministratorApr 24, 2026

Rethinking corporate structure

The article contrasts shareholder-focused governance with public-benefit corporations, which are designed to consider social objectives alongside financial performance.

Potential advantages

  1. Proactive compliance may help reduce some regulatory friction.
  2. A public-benefit commitment may strengthen brand trust and player loyalty.
  3. Long-term sustainability can be prioritized over short-term gains.
